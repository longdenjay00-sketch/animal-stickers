# African Lion

**Panthera leo** · IUCN Vulnerable (assessed 6 May 2025) · CITES Appendix II (Indian population Appendix I)

## Lore

In the night of the savanna the pride is a single animal with many throats. Lionesses hunt in a line of fire; the male’s mane is a shield and a flag. Ancient Egypt carved him into the sphinx and named the fierce goddess Sekhmet with a lioness head. Ashurbanipal of Assyria had himself carved killing lions so the court would see that the king could match the king of beasts. Medieval Europe put a gold lion on banners from Scotland to Ethiopia’s Solomonic line. In Swahili he is *simba*. The roar that carries eight kilometres is still the sound people use when they mean rule.

## Facts

- The only truly social big cat. Related females form a pride; coalitions of males hold tenure for about two to three years.
- Adult males 110–272 kg, head-body 172–250 cm; females smaller and the primary hunters.
- Unique among living felids for the male mane, which signals age, health, and fighting value and also cushions bites to the neck.
- Roars, scent marks, and visual displays keep neighbouring prides apart. Cubs of a new coalition are often killed, after which females come back into oestrus.
- Two living subspecies are now recognised: *Panthera leo leo* (West/Central Africa and Asia, Endangered) and *P. l. melanochaita* (East and Southern Africa, Vulnerable).
- Wild lifespan typically 12–18 years. Litter size 1–4 cubs. Females nurse one another’s young in a crèche.

## Known history

Lions once ranged from southern Europe and North Africa across southwest Asia to India. The Barbary lion of the Maghreb, the Cape lion, and the European cave-and-steppe lions are gone. Cave art at Chauvet and Lascaux already treats the animal as a being of power.

The 2025 IUCN assessment lists the species as **Vulnerable A2c**. Extant range is about **1,566,675 km²**, roughly **6% of historical range**, with an estimated **34% decline** over three generations (~21 years). The African Lion Database (unpublished 2025, cited by IUCN) puts **22,000–25,000** adult and subadult lions in Africa, plus about **670** in India’s Gir Forest — the last Asiatic lions. Habitat loss is the main driver; prey poaching and conflict with livestock keepers follow. Conservation has held or rebuilt some populations (Akagera in Rwanda, the Zambezi Delta in Mozambique). The Green Status score is **Largely Depleted** (30%).

India’s Asiatic lions are a conservation bottleneck: one population, one forest complex, high inbreeding risk. Africa’s remaining strongholds sit in well-funded parks of the east and south. West and Central African lions are far fewer and listed Endangered as *P. l. leo*.
