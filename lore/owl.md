# Barn Owl

**Tyto alba** · IUCN Least Concern

## Lore

The heart-shaped face is a dish antenna. Feathers on the leading edge of the wing break turbulence so the strike is silent. Farmers in Europe and Asia once nailed owls to barn doors; later they learned the bird was the reason the grain lasted. Ghost, hag, and wise-woman all got this white mask in folklore.

## Facts

- One of the most widely distributed land birds on Earth, absent mainly from polar and hyper-arid interiors.
- Asymmetrical ear openings and a facial disc give extreme auditory prey location in darkness.
- Feeds mostly on small mammals. A pair can take thousands of rodents a year.
- Nests in cavities, barns, and nest boxes. Does not build a stick nest.
- Global status Least Concern, but local declines follow loss of grassland and old buildings.

## Known history

Barn owls appear in Egyptian and European iconography as night spirits. Linnaeus placed related forms in early owl genera; *Tyto* is now its own family (Tytonidae), split from typical owls. Modern conservation is nest-box programs in farm country.
