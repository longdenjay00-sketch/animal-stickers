# Black Stallion

**Equus ferus caballus** · domestic horse, intact male · coat colour: black

## Lore

A stallion is not a breed. He is an adult male horse that has not been gelded, and the black coat is a genetic colour — a horse whose hair is true black from muzzle to tail, often with a blue sheen in the light. People have loaded that silhouette with story: Bucephalus of Alexander, the Friesian of the Low Countries, the Andalusian of the Iberian school, the desert sires that founded the Thoroughbred. In steppe epic the stallion is the second self of the rider. In this pack he rears as a cut-out because that is how banners remember him.

## Facts

- Domestic horses descend from the **DOM2** lineage. A stallion is specifically an intact male; geldings and mares are different roles in the same species.
- Black is a base coat colour (genotype requiring functional *ASIP* recessive / *MC1R* dominant combinations, depending on notation). Sun-bleach can turn a black horse brownish on the back.
- Shoulder height varies by breed from pony to 18-hand draught. A typical riding stallion is 15–17 hands.
- Socially, wild and feral bands are usually one stallion, a group of mares, and young. Bachelor groups of young males form until they can take a band.
- Gaits: walk, trot, canter, gallop. Some breeds add ambling gaits. Top gallop in racehorses exceeds 40 mph.
- Gestation ~11 months; foals stand within hours. Horses are hindgut fermenters of grass.

## Known history

Dogs, sheep, and cattle were domesticated thousands of years before the horse. Clear archaeological domestication appears in the mid-fourth millennium BCE; **modern domestic horses do not descend from the Botai culture horses of Kazakhstan (~3500 BCE)**. Ancient DNA (Orlando, Librado, and colleagues, *Nature* 2021) places the homeland of the DOM2 lineage in the **lower Volga–Don** region of the Pontic–Caspian steppe. Those horses spread explosively from about **2200–2000 BCE**, together with Sintashta spoke-wheeled chariots and a suite of locomotor and behaviour genes (*GSDMC*, *ZFPM1*).

Y-chromosome diversity collapsed over time: relatively few stallion lines sired the modern world’s horses, while many wild mares were folded in, which is why mitochondrial DNA stays diverse. The Thoroughbred of the English turf traces in the male line to three Oriental stallions imported around 1680–1730: the Byerley Turk, the Darley Arabian, and the Godolphin Arabian.

Przewalski’s horse is a sister lineage, not the ancestor of DOM2. The tarpan is extinct. Feral populations (mustangs, brumbies, Namib desert horses) are domestic horses living wild, not surviving wild ancestors.
