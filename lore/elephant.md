# African Savanna Elephant

**Loxodonta africana** · IUCN Endangered (assessed 13 November 2020, published 2022) · CITES Appendix I / II by population

## Lore

The matriarch carries a map in her head: water in drought, graves of kin, the smell of a season. Infrasound rolls through the ground so a herd miles away can answer. Both sexes may grow tusks; the ears are the shape of the continent. Carthage walked elephants over the Alps — most scholars now think those were the now-vanished North African population, not the Asian elephant. In many African cosmologies the elephant is memory and kingship. Ivory made it a commodity, and the commodity nearly unmade the animal.

## Facts

- Largest living land mammal. Bulls may exceed 6 tonnes and 3.2 m at the shoulder; cows are smaller.
- Recognised as a species separate from the African forest elephant (*Loxodonta cyclotis*) by IUCN in 2021. The two lineages split on the order of 2.5–5 million years ago.
- Matriarchal family units; males leave at adolescence and live in bachelor groups or alone.
- Tusks are ever-growing incisors used for digging, stripping bark, and fighting. Some populations have seen a rise in tusklessness under poaching pressure.
- Communication includes rumble, trumpet, seismic vibration, and chemical cues. Hearing and smell are acute; vision is secondary.
- Keystone browsers and grazers: they open woodland, seed-disperse, and dig water.
- Pregnancy ~22 months, the longest of any mammal. Calves are allomothered by aunts.

## Known history

Savanna elephants once ranged most of the African continent. They now persist in **24 countries**. Anecdotal early-1800s guesses ran as high as tens of millions; by the mid-1800s ivory and habitat loss had already cut that down. The African Elephant Status Report 2016 estimated about **415,428** African elephants of both species combined, and a drop of roughly **111,000** since 2006. The Great Elephant Census reported large savanna-elephant losses in the 2007–2014 window.

IUCN assesses *L. africana* as **Endangered A2bd**: more than **50% reduction** over three generations (~75 years), judged ongoing. Forest elephants are **Critically Endangered**. Regional exceptions exist — southern African populations in well-protected parks can be stable or increasing and are listed Least Concern on some national red lists — but the continental trend is down. Drivers: ivory poaching, habitat conversion, and human–elephant conflict. The 1989 CITES ivory ban, subsequent one-off sales, and national hunting policies have all left marks on numbers and on politics.

Hannibal’s elephants (218 BCE) and the war elephants of the Hellenistic world belong to this family memory even if the North African form is extinct. The living savanna elephant is what remains of that deep African lineage.
