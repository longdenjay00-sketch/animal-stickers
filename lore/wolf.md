# Arctic Wolf

**Canis lupus arctos** · a high-latitude subspecies of the grey wolf (*Canis lupus*, IUCN Least Concern)

## Lore

On the Queen Elizabeth Islands the sun fails for months and a white wolf still hunts muskox in the dark. Inuit stories treat the wolf as a partner in the same cold, not a villain. The sticker sits because Arctic wolves often do — waiting, watching, ears sharp against a teal sky.

## Facts

- Smaller and paler than many continental grey wolves; coat white to cream year-round.
- Lives in the Canadian Arctic Archipelago and parts of Greenland; isolated by sea ice and tundra from southern wolves.
- Pack hunters of muskox, Arctic hare, and caribou. Dens in rock outcrops because permafrost blocks digging.
- The species *Canis lupus* is Least Concern globally; local Arctic populations are naturally small and climate-sensitive.
- First described as a distinct form in the nineteenth century; taxonomy of wolf subspecies is still debated.

## Known history

Arctic wolves were among the last large carnivores to meet sustained European hunting pressure, because their islands were so remote. They remain a working model of wolves in a landscape with few people. Sea-ice loss and northward shifts of prey are the new history being written around them.
