# Bengal Tiger

**Panthera tigris tigris** · species IUCN Endangered (2021/2022) · CITES Appendix I

## Lore

Stripes are a name written on the body: no two tigers share a pattern. In the Sundarbans the tiger swims the tide; in Rajasthan it holds dry teak forest; in Bhutan camera traps have caught it near 4,300 metres. Hindu and Buddhist art give the tiger to Durga and to the arhat who rides one. Chinese poetry made it the counterpart of the dragon — earth against heaven. Colonial shikar turned the animal into a trophy and, by the late 1960s, into a ghost of the sal forest. Project Tiger (1973) is the modern epic: nine reserves became more than fifty, and India now holds about three quarters of all wild tigers.

## Facts

- Largest living cat in most of its remaining range. Males commonly 180–258 kg; females 100–167 kg; total length often 2.4–3.1 m.
- Solitary, territorial, mostly crepuscular and nocturnal. Prefers ungulates (sambar, chital, wild boar, barasingha).
- Habitat generalist: terai grassland, mangrove, evergreen and dry deciduous forest, even high mountain woodland.
- Each individual’s stripe pattern is unique and is used in camera-trap identification.
- Wild lifespan about 8–15 years; longer in managed care.
- The Bengal tiger is the nominate subspecies of *Panthera tigris* and the numerical core of the species.

## Known history

Tigers once ran from eastern Turkey to the Russian Far East and the Sunda islands. Bali, Javan, and Caspian tigers are extinct. The species now occupies well under 10% of historical range, with breeding populations in ten countries.

IUCN (assessed 15 December 2021, published 2022) lists *Panthera tigris* as **Endangered A2abcd**, with **2,608–3,905** mature individuals (best estimate **3,140**). The Green Status (2025) is **Critically Depleted**. India’s 2022 all-India tiger estimate averaged **3,682** (range 3,167–3,925). Nepal, Bhutan, and the Bangladesh Sundarbans add several hundred more Bengal tigers; a commonly cited regional total is on the order of **4,400**.

In 1972 India banned tiger hunting. In 1973 it launched Project Tiger. Poaching for skins and bones, habitat fragmentation, and conflict killings remain the pressure. Recovery is real inside well-protected source sites and thin outside them. About 35% of India’s reserves still need stronger protection.
