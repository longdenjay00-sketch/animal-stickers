# Jaguar

**Panthera onca** · IUCN Near Threatened

## Lore

In Maya and Aztec thought the jaguar is night sun, cave, and kingship. Its bite is built to crack turtle shell and caiman skull — a cat that kills through the head as often as the throat. Rosettes, not stripes: a gold field with black roses. It is the only living *Panthera* of the Americas.

## Facts

- Largest cat in the Americas. Stockier than a leopard, with a broader head and more powerful bite.
- Range once ran from the southern United States to northern Argentina; now patchy from Mexico through the Amazon to the Pantanal and Iberá.
- Swims readily. Hunts peccary, capybara, caiman, fish, and livestock where wild prey is thin.
- Melanistic (“black panther”) jaguars are locally common in dense forest.
- IUCN Near Threatened; Amazon basin still holds the core, but the Atlantic Forest and US populations are gone or tiny.

## Known history

Jaguars walked Pleistocene North America. The last U.S. breeding population faded in the twentieth century; individuals still occasionally cross from Sonora into Arizona. Jaguars were central to Olmec, Maya, and later Mexica iconography. Habitat fragmentation by ranches and roads, plus conflict killings, define the modern map. Jaguar corridors (for example through Belize, Brazil’s interior, and the Pantanal) are the recovery plan.
