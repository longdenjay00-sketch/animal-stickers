# Orca

**Orcinus orca** · IUCN Data Deficient globally (some populations Endangered)

## Lore

Sailors called it killer whale; the name stuck to a dolphin that takes minke whales, herring, and, in one culture, the tongues of great whales. Pods keep dialects and hunting recipes. A grandmother can lead for decades. The black-and-white flag of the dorsal fin is one of the ocean’s oldest family crests.

## Facts

- Largest dolphin (family Delphinidae). Males: tall triangular dorsal fin to ~1.8 m; females smaller and sickle-shaped.
- Cosmopolitan: polar to tropical seas. Distinct ecotypes (resident fish-eaters, transient mammal-eaters, offshore) do not interbreed where they overlap.
- Culture is real: vocal dialects, prey specialisation, and techniques (wave-washing seals, carousel feeding) are learned.
- Matrilineal pods. No global abundance estimate of high confidence, hence Data Deficient at species level.
- Southern Residents of the North Pacific are a famous Endangered population.

## Known history

Neolithic carvings and Indigenous Pacific art treat orcas as kin and as hunters. Twentieth-century live-capture for marine parks changed public feeling twice: first spectacle, then backlash. Current science treats orcas as multiple conservation units, not one interchangeable stock.
